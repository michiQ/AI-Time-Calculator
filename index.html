AI Time Calculator

Stop thinking like you're managing an organizational hierarchy. Start thinking like your business is facing an existential threat.

You don't have to change how your business operates today.

But what happens when AI changes your business tomorrow?

The systems, processes, and decision-making structures that work perfectly well today may become expensive obstacles when you need to move quickly.

The question isn't what your organization costs to run. It's what your organization will cost to change.

This calculator focuses on one part of that challenge: how quickly leaders can see development work across the business, understand the impact of a new priority, and redirect teams. It does not assess executive finance decisions or the cost of transforming an entire company.

THE THREE QUESTIONS

How long does it take to respond?

Enter the time your organization needs today, from a new strategic priority being identified to development teams beginning work. These are your estimates, not an industry benchmark.

1 · Understand current commitments

What are our development teams working on, and why?

Time to see roadmaps and Jira work across organizations, and connect work to enterprise goals, products, and initiatives.

2 · Understand the impact of change

If a new priority comes in, what work and commitments would be affected?

Time to identify affected products, initiatives, dependencies, roadmaps, and capacity. Include portfolio review and stage gates only where they actually add delay.

3 · Redirect development work

How quickly can we make trade-offs and get teams started on the new priority?

Time from understanding the trade-offs to agreeing on priorities and getting development teams started. Do not include the full product build or launch.

The dropdowns help explain why delays happen. The weeks you enter determine the time estimate, so we don't invent delays or count the same problem twice.

FINANCIAL ASSUMPTIONS

What might that delay mean financially?

For example, connecting systems, establishing goal/product/initiative relationships, adjusting portfolio reporting, and training teams. Enter a planning estimate; this is not calculated from company revenue.

Use an estimated monthly profit contribution or measurable net business benefit, not gross sales. For an opportunity, this could be new profit you could earn. For a threat, profit you could preserve. Leave at $0 if you cannot reasonably estimate it.

A conservative assumption helps account for work that can happen in parallel and dependencies outside the development organization.

How are the numbers calculated?

Current time: sum of the three current-week estimates. Improved time: sum of the three improved-week estimates. This assumes the stages happen sequentially; if your teams work in parallel, enter elapsed time for each stage without double counting.

Time gained: current minus improved, never below zero. Potential business value: time gained ÷ 4.33 weeks per month × monthly net business value × percentage of time gained that moves the business outcome, provided the checkbox is selected.

Cost 1: your estimated one-time improvement cost. Cost 2: potential value exposed to avoidable delay. These are different measures, and their sum is a scenario exposure, not money automatically spent or lost.

The model does not assume that connecting tools creates extra development capacity, that all portfolio processes are broken, or that faster starts always produce faster launches.

What does this mean for your business?

The goal isn't to replace board-level strategy or financial governance. It's to make the strategic goals that emerge from those processes visible across products, initiatives, teams, and Jira delivery work.

Visibility first. Then leaders can assess the consequences of change and make informed trade-offs. Simpler portfolio processes may help too, but only where they are actually slowing decisions.

Examples such as Zayo and Disney can illustrate different patterns of the same visibility challenge. Neither company's financial impact is inferred from this illustrative calculator.
